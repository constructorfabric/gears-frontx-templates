# When to take or offer the step

The proposal step comes before a new page or screen is built, on whatever route builds it. It builds nothing itself and never replaces the build. Decide it by meaning, in whatever language the person writes:

- **Asked for.** The person asked to see the page, or options for it, before it is built: take the step at once, with no further question.
- **Offered once.** Otherwise, when new pages or screens are about to be built and the person has neither seen nor described how they look, offer the step once: one short line that covers all of them, in the person's own language. Never offer again for a screen already offered or declined.
- **Taken only on a yes.** The offer ends the agent's turn: nothing is built in that turn, and the agent waits for the person's reply. A reply that accepts takes the step. A reply that does not accept it (a refusal, a wish to build at once, or a reply that ignores the offer) means build now, with no second ask.
- **No offer.** The person already gave a design, a mockup or an exact description, or the change is small: a fix, one more field, a text change.

The offer carries a meaning, not a fixed sentence: before building, the agent can first draw the new screens on one page, with options where a real choice exists, for the person to choose from; if the person does not take it, the agent builds. Write it fresh, in the person's words and language, at the end of the message that says what is about to be built, and build nothing until the person replies.

Only the person chooses an option; the agent never picks one for them.

Everything the step shows the person is in the person's own language, the one they write in: the offer, the framing questions, the page (`page-parts.md`, "The page's language") and the message sent with it.
